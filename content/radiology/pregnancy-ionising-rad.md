

Pelvic irradiation = between diaphragm and knees


Fetal doses ≤ 1 mGy 
- 1 in 10,000 risk of childhood cancer
- natural risk is 1 in 500 (National Registry of Childhood Cancers 1991 - 2000)
- Includes XR pelvis
- Go ahead if clinically justified. CT head is ok.

Intermediate (1 - 10 mGy) CT abdomen, XR lumbar spine

High-dose = fetal dose > 10 mGy (any CT involving pelvis, fluoroscopy, whole body FDG-PET)
- 1 in 200 at most, usually < 1 in 1000
- Worst case scenario is doubling of childhood cancer risk compared to natural rate
- Think twice

Risk of hereditary disease from diagnostic procedures is negligible.

< 100 mGy, no significant deterministic effects.
Diagnostic procedures don’t result in fetal doses > 100 mGy.
No risk of fetal death, malformation, growth or mental retardation. 

Stochastic effects are independent of stage of pregnancy after first 4 weeks of gestation. Murky evidence before this period, but probably lower cancer risk than after first 4 weeks.

TOP not justified following any diagnostic exposure, even high-dose.

IV iodinated contrast - heel prick test to exclude congenital hypothyroidism.

Gadolinium not recommended in pregnancy.
