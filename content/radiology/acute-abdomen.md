# Acute appendicitis
- Appendix diameter (> 6 mm is abnormal)
- Aetiology: appendicolith, mass
- Perforation
- Abscess: size and location
- Gas within inflamed appendix - ?gangrenous appendicitis

# Small bowel obstruction
- Diameter > 2.5 cm and transition point
- High-grade (complete) or low-grade (partial)
- Faecalisation implies gradual onset with slow transit, may be amenable to conservative management
- Closed loop (C-shaped), ischaemia (hypoenhancement, mesenteric fluid, wall thickening/thinning), perforation

# Large bowel obstruction
- Long segment (> 10 cm) vs short segment (< 10 cm) obstructing segment; longer segment more likely nonmalignant
- Synchronous lesions
- Caecum most common site of perforation (diameter > 12 cm high risk of perforation)

# Diverticulitis
- Diverticula, wall thickening, fat stranding
- Complications: perforation, abscess, peritonitis, obstruction
- Malignancy: very enlarged nodes, distant nodes
- Hinchey classification to determine management

# Gastroduodenal perforation
- Wall: defect, thickening, ulceration
- Pneumoperitoneum location: upper abdomen in upper GI perforation (plus more fluid than in lower GI), lesser sac or along ligamentum teres in gastroduodenal perforation
- Abscess, malignancy

# Mesenteric ischaemia
- Involvement of other organs suggests embolic cause
- Look in the cardiac chambers for thrombus
- Arterial ischaemia: wall thickening due to oedema (hypodense) or intramural haemorrhage (hyperdense), when prolonged bowel becomes dilated (no peristalsis) and “paper-thin”
- Mesenteric oedema more associated with venous rather than arterial occlusion

# Cholecystitis
- US: Wall thickening > 3 mm, distension, pericholecystic fluid, hyperaemia, sonographic Murphy. Gas has dirty shadowing.
- CT: Wall thickening, fat stranding, +/- gallstones (may be occult on CT)
- Complications: gangrenous (wall hypoenhancement, sloughing - looks like intraluminal hyperdense linear bands arising from wall), emphysematous, perforation (abscesses, dropped gallstones)
- Prolonged cholecystitis -> cystic artery pseudoaneurysm -> haemorrhagic cholecystitis

# AAA rupture
- Size, morphology, relation to renal arteries, relation to iliac arteries, thrombosis extent
- Impending rupture: disrupted calcification, inflammatory stranding, hyperdense cresent, thrombus fissuring, large/rapidly enlarging

# Necrotising soft tissue infection
- Compartments, length, fascial planes (superficial, extraperitoneal, peritoneal), collections
