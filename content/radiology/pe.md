## Acute vs chronic

Acute
- Acute angle
- Thrombosed pulmonary artery is dilated
- Polo mint, central

Chronic
- Obtuse angle
- Thrombosed pulmonary artery is smaller
- Contrast recanalisation
- Web, eccentric
- Mosaic attenuation

## Right ventricular dysfunction

- Use “right ventricular dysfunction” instead of “right heart strain”
- RV/LV ratio in axial plane at largest diameters is the best predictor of adverse outcome (5-fold increase in 30-day mortality if > 1, but no association with long-term mortality)
- Septal bowing, dilated pulmonary trunk, dilated SVC and azygos, dilated RA
- Contrast reflux into IVC is meh. Low injection rate (< 3 mL/s) = high specificity, low sensitivity; high injection rate = high sensitivity, low specificity.

## Pitfalls
- Transient interruption of contrast (mixed with unopacified IVC blood)
- Ballpark minimum for diagnostic opacification is 250 HU in pulmonary trunk.

## Misc
- Describe most proximal location of PE.
- Avoid using “massive” and “submassive” PE (these refer to haemodynamic status).
- Clinical significance of subsegmental PE is controversial, but should be reported.
